## Why this one

I like lightweight merino layers for their comfort and odor resistance, but durability has often been the weak point. The Vapor Hoody feels substantial enough for frequent wear without losing the breathable feel and stink proofing I’m looking for.

The simple design also suits how I use it. There are no pockets, thumb holes, or hood adjustments to manage. It layers easily under a jacket and the relaxed hood and minimal branding work well for everyday wear and travel.

## In use

After more than 50 days of backcountry use, the fabric has handled pack straps better than I’ve come to expect from lightweight pure merino. I’ve seen relatively little abrasion at the shoulders and upper back, which makes me more comfortable wearing it regularly under a pack. More recently, I’ve used this more around town and the house, as I the Springbok hoodie has supplanted it for my backcountry kit.

Around town, it’s comfortable against bare skin and breathes well during movement, particularly in mild or cool conditions. Odor resistance is another reason I bring it traveling, where I want to wear layers repeatedly between washes. The sleeves push up easily, and the lack of bulky features keeps it comfortable under other clothing.

The reason I’ve shifted away from it in the backcountry is that while the hood adds some warmth and coverage in light wind, it blows back in stronger gusts. Without a closure or adjustment, I rely on a shell hood over it when conditions demand more protection.

## What I’d change

I’d add a simple way to secure the hood in wind. A small closure at the neck or increased fabric around the lip of the hood could make it more useful outside while preserving the relaxed fit I like for everyday wear.
